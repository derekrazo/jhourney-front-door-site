Mini-guide

A day on retreat, hour by hour

"What does a day actually look like?" is the question we get most on calls. Here is a typical Day 3, hour by hour, in each format. It's not arrival day, and it's not the last day. Exact times shift a little by venue and time zone, but the shape stays the same: two group sessions, one check-in with your facilitator, and a lot of your own practice in between. You plan that practice yourself, with help.

2

Group sessions a day

One in the morning and one in the evening, roughly 1-1.5 hours each. That's about 3 hours of group time a day.

This includes the group sessions, walking and lying-down practice. It's a suggestion, not a quota.

What people actually log mid-retreat

Median self-reported hours on Days 3-5 in nightly journals from three 2025 retreats (194 entries). The middle half logged 4-6 hours.

30 min

Shortest sit we suggest

Longer is welcome, but we'd rather curiosity pulls you into a long sit than willpower pushes you through one.

~20 min

Facilitator check-ins

Daily early in the week, then at least every other day. On work-compatible retreats, check-ins move to small groups of 3-4 after the first days.

None

First wake-up bell

The first group session is around 8-9 a.m. local time. Naps are encouraged.

When first jhanas tend to come

Of the 619 people with a recorded first-jhana day, 56.9% had reached it by the end of Day 3, and 43.1% got there on Day 4 or later. Across all 1,436 participants with a known outcome, 58.7% reached jhana: 60.1% in person and 57.5% online.

The practical stuff

What people ask about the day

Is it silent?

Mostly, outside the group sessions and your 1:1s. On full retreat days we ask you not to chat casually, and meals are usually quiet. You can still ask questions, share in groups, read and journal. Things loosen up later in the week with a social dinner and some partner meditations. On work-compatible weekdays you go about your life as normal.

What about my phone?

On in-person and online full-time retreats, we strongly encourage you to unplug for the whole week. People who keep checking email often tell us afterwards that they wish they hadn't. On work-compatible retreats, unplug on the retreat weekends and keep weekday screen time down. Recordings come on an MP3 player or in the course library, so you don't need your phone to practice.

I can't sit for 6 hours. Is that a problem?

No. Walking counts, lying down counts, and so do guided recordings. Sit in whatever posture is comfortable. Plenty of people end the week practicing mostly lying down. The suggestion is sits of 30 minutes or more with breaks between them, and most people log around 5 hours on a mid-retreat day.

Will I get enough sleep?

Yes. That's deliberate. There's no 4 a.m. wake-up, and we'd rather you nap than meditate exhausted. If you fall asleep during a lying-down session, treat it as information about your practice, not as a failure.

What's the food like?

In person, all meals are included and the venue cooks, usually with options for most diets. Online, you feed yourself, so plan simple meals ahead. Either way, don't make big changes to your diet or caffeine for the week, and please don't fast.

How much time do I get with a teacher?

You're assigned a facilitator who follows your progress all week. You'll have roughly 20-minute 1:1s, daily at first and then at least every other day. There are also two group sessions with Q&A every day, and your facilitator reads your nightly journal. Some facilitators also run drop-in office hours.

Is there yoga, breathwork or exercise?

In person, usually yes: optional yoga, sometimes breathwork, and access to walking trails or a gym, depending on the venue. Online, move however suits you. If you skip exercise, we ask you to do a couple of hours of walking meditation instead.

What if I'm not in a US time zone?

Online retreats work best for the Americas, Europe and Africa. When enough people are in Europe, an EU facilitator leads their morning session. It covers the same material the US group gets that evening. From Asia the hours are hard, and we usually don't recommend it.

Do I have to attend everything?

Please plan to. It's a full program, and we ask people to sign up only if they can do the whole thing. If you'll miss a session, tell your facilitator. We check in if someone goes quiet.

Is a hard day normal?

Very. Grief, boredom, restlessness and frustration come up for most people at some point in the week. That's part of the process, not a sign it isn't working. That's what the 1:1s are for. Many first jhanas arrive on Day 4 or later.
