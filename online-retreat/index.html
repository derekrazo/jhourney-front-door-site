Why it works at home

What home gives you that a retreat center can't.

Jhana happens in the privacy of your own mind. What moves it is practice and feedback, and those don't change. What changes is where you learn it.

01

You learn it where you'll use it.

The skill gets built into your real days, in your own room, around your own routines. It isn't left behind at a venue when you fly home.

53% of those who reached jhana could get back in reliably by the end (in person: 50%)

02

No crash on Monday.

In-person retreats end with a jolt back into normal life. Here there's no cliff: you've already spent a week practising around work, email and people talking to you.

54% called it life-changing (in person: 48%)

03

Time to let it click.

Between the two weekends you get workdays to try things, notice what happens and bring it back to your teacher. Practice spread over time tends to stick better than practice crammed together.

59.5% of first-timers reached jhana (in person: 57.6%)

Online is at least as high as in person on every measure we track. The gaps are small, so we read them as "just as good", not "better".

A retreat day

Six of the 11 days. About 8 hours of practice in all.

  1. Morning Live group session: teaching and a guided sit
  2. Late morning Your own sits, walks and rest
  3. During the day Check-in with your facilitator, one-to-one or in your small group
  4. Afternoon Your own sits and the day's experiment
  5. Evening Live group session: questions and a guided sit
  6. Night A short journal entry your facilitator reads

A workday

The four days in between. Practice on your own schedule.

  1. Before work About 1–1.5 hours of practice
  2. Daytime Work as normal
  3. After work About 1–1.5 hours of practice
  4. Once in the week A short check-in with your facilitator and small group
  5. Any time Optional office hours; every session is recorded

Live sessions run on US Pacific time. Europe gets its own adjusted schedule, and every session is recorded.

Making home work

Turn home into a retreat.

People who do well from home mostly do the same few things.

  • Tell the people you live with your hours. Undisturbed time on retreat days. Meals together are fine.
  • Put your phone away. Some people block everything but Zoom for the weekends, or have someone hide their phone.
  • Have one person who can reach you, so you don't need to check messages just in case.
  • Cook ahead, so meals don't eat into the weekends.
  • Set up two places to sit, and know where you'll walk.
  • Clear both weekends completely. No half-days, no evening plans.

Want the getaway too?

Some people book a cabin or a rental near nature for the retreat weekends and join from there. You get some of the leave-it-all-behind feeling without taking the week. All you need is a solid internet connection.

Questions

What people ask us

Does online really work as well as in person?

On our numbers, yes. 56.3% of people on our online retreats reached jhana (192 of 341), against 57.8% at a retreat center (329 of 569). That gap is well inside the noise.

It makes sense when you look at what drives it: the practice, and the feedback on it. Those are the same either way. Who's sitting in the room next to you matters less than people expect.

Is it all evenings? How much time do the workdays take?

No. The two weekends are full retreat days, Friday to Sunday, with about 8 hours of practice each day.

On the four workdays in between you practise for about 1–1.5 hours before work and again after, whenever suits you. There's one short evening meeting with your facilitator and small group. Office hours are optional, and every session is recorded.

I already live on Zoom. Will screens get in the way?

Less than you'd think. Group sessions are on Zoom, but most of the retreat is your own sits, eyes closed, away from any screen. You can put the laptop in another room between sessions.

What if I'm not in the US?

Live sessions are based on US Pacific time. Europe gets its own adjusted schedule, and every session is recorded.

From Asia the hours are hard, and we usually don't recommend it. Book a call and we'll look at your time zone with you.

I have kids. Can I make it work?

Parents do it, but it cuts both ways. For some, two clear weekends are easier to arrange than a week away. For others, a week away is easier than protecting two weekends at home. It's worth talking through on a call.

What happens after the retreat?

You join our community of people who've been on a retreat. And because you've already been practising around work, there's no gap between the retreat and the rest of your life: you just keep going.
