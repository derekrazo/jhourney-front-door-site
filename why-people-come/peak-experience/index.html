How it works for this

The memory is the trap.

Chasing a remembered peak adds the very tension that blocks it. What got you in last time was the letting go, not the place you let go into. So the practice starts from scratch, small, and lets the memory be a sign that it's possible rather than a target.

  1. 01

    Start as if it never happened

    A small pleasant feeling, here and now. Not the peak, just the first warm step.

  2. 02

    Thank the memory, let it go

    When it shows up and you start comparing, notice it kindly and come back to what's here.

  3. 03

    Measure warmth, not height

    The only question is how warmly you can relate to this moment. That's what lets the loop build.

What jhana is, in one paragraph

The jhanas are states of deep joy and calm that you build on purpose. You start from a small pleasant feeling, like a smile or a warm memory, and rest your attention on it. Attention makes the feeling stronger, and the stronger feeling holds attention, until the loop sustains itself. It works like a panic attack in reverse, and it's learned by relaxing, not by concentrating harder.

What it won't do, and who it isn't for

What it won't do

  • Having been there before helps, but it's no guarantee: about 1 in 5 people who'd reached jhana before didn't in the week.
  • About half of those who reach jhana leave with reliable access. The rest need practice afterwards.
  • It may not feel like your original peak. Many people describe jhana as quieter and clearer than the moment that brought them here.

Who it isn't for

  • We screen everyone before they book. A retreat isn't the right time during an acute crisis or with unmanaged mental-health conditions, and it doesn't replace therapy or treatment.

What share of people can reliably get back into jhana afterwards?

About half of the people who reach jhana leave confident they can get back in, within an hour, whenever they want.

If it feels that good, couldn’t I get hooked?

Pleasure and addiction aren’t the same thing. Jhana is closer to the pleasure of a good friendship or a runner’s high than to a slot machine, and you can’t get in by craving it.

Full answer →

What happens if I’m in the unlucky 40%?

It happens. About 4 in 10 people don’t reach jhana in the week. Many still leave with something they value, but not all of them, and they rate the week lower.
