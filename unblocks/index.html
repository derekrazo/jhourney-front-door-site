Unblocks

Six guided recordings from the retreat. One for each place people most often get stuck.

Each one is a complete guided sit from the Jhourney retreat curriculum, chosen for a single sticking point. Pick the one that sounds like you, press play, and notice what happens instead of judging it. Then answer the three questions underneath. That’s where most of the learning lands.

Where are you stuck?

  1. “I try so hard and nothing happens” Half the effort · 16 min
  2. “My mind won’t stop talking” Brush it with kindness · 15 min
  3. “I don’t feel anything pleasant at all” A seedling of gratitude · 13 min
  4. “The good feeling fades as soon as I notice it” Sink into it · 14 min
  5. “I keep waiting for something to happen” Not knowing · 23 min
  6. “Hard emotions come up when I sit” Forgiveness · 26 min

01Use headphones. Several of these have long quiet stretches. They’re on purpose, not a glitch.

02Sit or lie down, whichever lets you relax without nodding off.

03Don’t grade the sit. You’re running an experiment, and a sit where nothing happened is still data.

04One a day is plenty. Give the same recording a few days before you switch.

01 / 06

“I try so hard and nothing happens”

Half the effort

Guided by Klau · 16 minutes

Klau has you smile, then smile again at half the effort, then half of that, down to the lightest smile you can manage, before trying it with your heart, your eyes and your whole experience. You finish by letting the faintest smile grow slowly into a big, goofy grin.

Why it helpsEffort adds the very tension the practice is trying to release. Halving it again and again shows you, in your own body, how little it takes. An easy, honest smile tends to last longer than a forced one.

Before you press play

  • Notice how much effort you were about to bring, and agree to drop half of it.
  • At the start Klau mentions a group debrief afterwards. This page is your debrief. To go straight in, skip to 1:06.

0:0016:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. Which smile felt best? Was it the biggest, or one of the smallest?
  2. Did any smile make you feel worse? If so, what was the instruction really asking for, underneath the words?
  3. The right smile can change from day to day. How will you find tomorrow’s?

NextIf an easy smile showed up, try Sink into it to stay with it. If effort kept creeping back, try the troubleshooter’s “Cut it in half” experiment, which is the written version of this.

02 / 06

“My mind won’t stop talking”

Brush it with kindness

Guided by Stephen · 15 minutes

Stephen has you breathe out slowly, let the mind fall wide open and relax a few spots you didn’t know were tense. Then you brush whatever turns up with a little kindness and let it pass: sounds, thoughts, boredom, numbness, even the sense of a “you” doing it.

Why it helpsDistractions aren’t the enemy. How you meet them is. Each one that ends in kindness and relaxation, rather than suppression, is a rep in the right direction, and this sit is fifteen minutes of those reps.

Before you press play

  • At about 7:50 Stephen leaves you on your own for a couple of minutes. The quiet is part of it, so keep brushing.
  • You don’t need a quiet mind. You only need something to brush.

0:0015:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. When a distraction arrived, did you brace against it or embrace it? What did each one feel like in your body?
  2. Did anything shift or move on once you stopped pushing it away?
  3. Did you notice more going on than usual? What if that counts as sharper awareness rather than a worse sit?

NextIf a pleasant feeling showed up once things calmed down, try Sink into it. If the chatter got louder instead of softer, start with Half the effort. Tension often drives the talking.

03 / 06

“I don’t feel anything pleasant at all”

A seedling of gratitude

Guided by Klau · 13 minutes

After a slow, softening body scan, Klau has you find a small bright spot in your heart: a seedling holding all the gratitude you’ve ever felt. You watch it crack open, put down roots and grow into a tree and then a forest, seen through the eyes of yourself at six years old.

Why it helpsYou’re not waiting for fireworks. Gratitude is often easier to find than joy, and a vivid, playful image gives a faint warm feeling something to grow on.

Before you press play

  • You don’t need to see anything. A loose sense of the image is plenty, because the feeling is what you’re after.
  • If the image pulls you into a story, let the story go and come back to how it feels.

0:0013:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. Which part landed most: the everyday things, the people, the meals, or the places your legs have taken you?
  2. Was gratitude easier to find than joy? How was it different: softer, warmer, more about other people?
  3. By the end, had the gratitude turned into anything else, like gladness or ease?

NextIf you found even a flicker, try Sink into it to stay with it. If images aren’t your thing, try Half the effort, since a smile is the simplest place to start.

04 / 06

“The good feeling fades as soon as I notice it”

Sink into it

Guided by a Jhourney teacher · 14 minutes

You ask, “What is there in experience that I can enjoy right now?”, find something small, and sink into it. You sink forward as if gently pulled by a string, then backwards as if floating in water, then straight down like a slow elevator, resting between rounds.

Why it helpsGrabbing at a fading feeling adds tension and drains it faster. Sinking gives your attention something soft to keep doing, so you stay with the feeling as it changes instead of chasing it.

Before you press play

  • This one comes from an earlier retreat, and the sound is a little rough, so turn it up a notch.
  • When you can’t sink any further, stop. That’s the cue to relax, not to push.

0:0014:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. Which direction felt most natural: forward, backwards or down?
  2. When you stopped sinking and just rested, did the feeling vanish, or did something like relaxation take its place?
  3. Which came more easily today, enjoying or relaxing? Could you lead with that one next time?

NextIf the feeling stayed a little longer, try Not knowing to loosen the urge to check on it. If there was nothing to sink into, start with A seedling of gratitude.

05 / 06

“I keep waiting for something to happen”

Not knowing

Guided by Jack · 23 minutes

Jack keeps asking, “What is happening right now?” and “Did you know it would feel like this?” Then you whisper “I don’t know” to any tension you find, and you finish with it as a quiet phrase on each breath.

Why it helpsHolding the goal tightly keeps you checking, and checking is tension. Noticing over and over that you couldn’t have predicted this moment loosens the scorecard and lets curiosity do the work.

Before you press play

  • If you catch yourself checking whether it’s working, that’s fine. That’s what the questions are for.
  • Partway through, Jack mentions being moved to tears by beauty, not by distress. Nothing has to happen. It’s only an invitation.

0:0023:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. What came up in this sit that you couldn’t have predicted?
  2. When you whispered “I don’t know” to a tense spot, what happened to it?
  3. Did you feel more awake or less by the end? Where in your day could you ask, “What’s happening right now?”

NextIf this loosened the checking, try Sink into it next. If it stirred up something heavy, try Forgiveness.

06 / 06

“Hard emotions come up when I sit”

Forgiveness

Guided by Vlad · 26 minutes

Vlad starts with relaxation and a small smile, then the phrase “I forgive myself for not understanding.” From there you offer forgiveness to people who hurt you and to situations that didn’t go your way, and finally you imagine someone you hurt forgiving you.

Why it helpsWhen hard feelings surface, the trap is relaxing away from them. Forgiveness is a way of relaxing into them, and it’s the tool our teachers reach for most when emotions block a sit.

Before you press play

  • Forgiving isn’t condoning. As Vlad puts it, you don’t have to forgive; you get to. If nothing comes, forgive yourself for that.
  • If your heart races or you feel panic or numbness, stop and do something grounding. If it doesn’t settle, talk to a professional.

0:0026:00

Afterwards, ask yourself

  1. Which was easiest: forgiving yourself, someone else, or a situation?
  2. Where in your body did you feel it, and does anything feel lighter now?
  3. Did an “I should forgive” show up? What happened when you forgave that too?

NextIf you feel lighter, try Sink into it while your heart is open. If it was too much, read the window-of-tolerance notes on the troubleshooter before you sit again.
