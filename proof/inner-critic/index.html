Loving the younger versions of yourself

Day 6 of an online retreat (Dec 2024). The participant says that when they meditate they picture themselves as a baby or a teenager and try to love those versions of themselves, and that this afternoon was the first time they had ever been able to.

What people say changed

  • People describe turning toward the critic instead of arguing with it, and the critic softening.
  • They notice how much background tension and low-level anxiety they had been carrying, and find some of it can be let go.
  • Self-compassion becomes a move you can practise. Several people who didn't reach jhana described it as a highlight of their week.
  • Calm stops being something you force.
  • In our 2024 alumni survey, feeling less "nervous, anxious or on edge" was among the biggest self-reported changes.

What it won’t do

  • This is training, not treatment, and it doesn't replace care for an anxiety disorder. We screen for recent panic attacks, ongoing depression and some other conditions before anyone attends.
  • A week of trying can feed the critic. Some people spend days comparing themselves with people who 'got in'.
  • Difficult feelings sometimes come up before ease does. Staff logged 38 adverse-effect cases across 1,491 participant-retreats (2.5%), most often emotional dysregulation or passing fear, and mostly lasting hours or days; 31 are marked resolved.
  • Changes can fade. Several people said it was too early to tell whether it would last.
