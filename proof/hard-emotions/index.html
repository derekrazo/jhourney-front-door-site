A deep reset

Day 6 of an online retreat. In a deep jhana, they noticed tension they hadn’t known was there and spent twenty minutes letting it dissolve. The next day: “so much more alive.” 56 seconds.

What people say changed

  • Old grief and resentment move. People describe long cries and forgiving others and themselves.
  • Much of it comes from the forgiveness and emotional-processing practices before jhana, so it doesn't depend on reaching jhana.
  • People leave with a way to keep processing at home, rather than a one-off event.
  • Some compare it to months of therapy compressed into a week. Those are their words, not our claim, and at least one credits their earlier therapy for making it possible.

What it won’t do

  • It doesn't erase the past. As our FAQ puts it, "the jhanas don't magically make one's past psychological conditioning disappear."
  • It can be uncomfortable. Stored fear and sadness can come up for hours or days. Emotional dysregulation was the most common logged adverse effect (12 of 38 cases, across 1,491 participant-retreats).
  • Forgiveness practice can be painful and lonely the first time. Tell a facilitator when it is; that's what they're there for.
  • Not everyone gets a release. Some spend days with tears that don't resolve into anything.
  • It isn't a substitute for therapy or trauma treatment, and it's not the right time if you're in an acute crisis or have unmanaged mental-health conditions. We screen for this.
