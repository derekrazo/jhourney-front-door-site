In their words

What jhana feels like

In 399 people’s own words

On the last day of every retreat we ask everyone the same question: what’s a relatable experience you’d compare your peak to? Between October 2024 and August 2025, 399 people answered. Here are about 130 of those answers, lightly copyedited, including the people who said “nothing” and the ones who were underwhelmed.

  1. “Saying ‘I love you’ for the first time, but at 10x the intensity.”
    Love and being held
  2. “Psychedelics, but with the additional knowledge that there is no distortion from a substance. This is truly in reach within ourselves on ~an hour’s notice.”
    Psychedelics, minus the substance
  3. “Like being in a hot tub, while a world-class massage therapist is rubbing you, while opening a jar that’s been stuck, while rocking your sleeping child, while your belly is full of soup, while being pleasantly tired after a workout.”
    Deep rest and safety
  4. “People who can’t see color seeing a sunset for the first time. People who can’t hear hearing for the first time.”
    Awe, beauty and wonder
  5. “Like a distilled and amplified version of the feeling of relief you get after you’re crying and someone is holding you and the feelings have passed, so everything feels okay.”
    A good cry, and the relief after
  6. “First time skiing down a black diamond and just crushing it. No fear, no hesitation, perfect control. Like ‘Holy shit, I’m doing it. I’m good!’”
    Flow, mastery and breakthroughs

How good was it?

As good as or better than the best thing that happened to you in the past…

All 407 people who filled in the exit survey answered this, whether or not they reached jhana. 133 of them (32.7%) put their peak at a year or more. Among the 229 who answered a plain “yes” to having reached jhana, it was 104 (45.4%).

What would you compare it to?

128 of their answers, in 8 themes

About these answers
  • Jhourney exit surveys, October 2024 to August 2025: 407 respondents across 15 retreats (internal file “260212 Consolidated exit surveys”, first sheet). No names or contact details are used.
  • Comparisons: “What’s a relatable experience you would compare this peak experience to?” (399 substantive answers out of 407).
  • Peak moments: “In a sentence or so, what would you describe as your peak experience on this retreat?”
  • Ladder: “Was your peak experience as good or better than the best thing that happened to you that hour, day, week, month, year, or longer?” (n=407). Same figures as the exit-survey cross-check in our retreat statistics; across all post-retreat data (n=976) the year-or-more share is 33.2%.
  • Jhana: self-reported (“Based on the way they’ve been described, do you feel like you experienced a jhana on this retreat?”), not staff-judged.

Jhourney teaches the jhanas: states of deep joy and calm, learned in days rather than decades.
