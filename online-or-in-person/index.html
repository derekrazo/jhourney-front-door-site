In person or online?

If you're choosing between one of our retreats in person and our online retreat that fits around work, this is what we've learned from running both. There's good in each, and online is not the lesser version. Which one fits depends on you.

Does online work as well?

It's the first thing almost everyone asks, and a fair one. Most people expect the at-home version to do worse. Here's what our data shows.

The gap is 1.5 points, well inside the noise for groups this size, and smaller than the difference between one good week and another in either format. We've found online works just as well.

Among people who had never reached jhana before, the at-home group did slightly better: 59.5%, against 57.6% at a retreat center.

02

What's the same

It's the same retreat, moved to a different place. The schedule carries over nearly block for block.

  • The exercises. The same curriculum and the same daily experiments.
  • The facilitators. A facilitator follows your progress all the way through and reads your nightly journal.
  • The group sessions. Teaching, guided sits and questions, morning and evening on retreat days.
  • The feedback. Each day you look at what happened in your sits, find where you're stuck, and pick the next thing to try.

Why do the results match? Jhana happens in the privacy of your own mind. What moves it is the practice and the feedback on it, which are the same in both. Who's sitting in the room next to you matters less than people expect.

03

What's different: who holds the space

At a retreat center, the place holds the retreat for you. At home, you hold it yourself. Both have real strengths.

In person, at a retreat center

7 days away

Where it wins

  • You leave your life behind. New place, no chores, no one needing you. For many people that's the easiest way to go all in.
  • Everything is handled. Room and meals are taken care of, and the day is laid out for you.
  • A room of people doing the same thing. Shared sits, a facilitator to talk to over lunch, a social dinner later in the week.
  • The setting. Nature, walking trails, and usually optional yoga.

What it asks of you

  • A full week off, plus travel.
  • Fixed dates at a few venues, which fill up.

Online, around your work

about 11 days, two Fridays off

Where it wins

  • No week off. Two retreat weekends with ordinary workdays in between.
  • You learn it where you'll keep using it. The skill gets built into your real life, not left behind at a venue.
  • No jarring return. You're already practicing around work, so there's no cliff on Monday. And you get more days to let it settle.
  • Your own space. Your bed, your food. Some people find a familiar room easier to open up in than a new place full of strangers.

What it asks of you

  • You hold the boundaries yourself: two whole weekends kept clear, and people at home who give you room.
  • Group time is on video calls.

How the 11 days work

A common misreading is that the work-compatible retreat happens in the evenings. It doesn't. The weekends are full retreat days, and the weekdays fit around your job.

A retreat day

Retreat days look like a day at the retreat center: a morning and an evening group session on video, a check-in with your facilitator, and your own sits and walking in between. Sessions run on US Pacific time.

A workday

On workdays you practice for about an hour to an hour and a half before work and again in the evening, then write a short journal entry. Once during the week you meet your facilitator with three or four others for about 20 minutes.

05

Which fits you

There's no right answer, but these questions sort most people quickly.

Leans in personLeans online

Would you be more distracted at home, or away from it?

In personHome is full of things that need you.

OnlineNew places and new people keep you on edge.

Can you protect two whole weekends, Friday to Sunday?

In personWeekends are your hardest days to clear.

OnlineA week off is harder than two Fridays.

Will the people you live with give you room?

In personNot really, or you live with small kids and no backup.

OnlineYes, if you agree on hours.

Can you leave work email alone on the retreat days?

In personOnly if you're physically away.

OnlineYes, with your phone in a drawer.

What do you want to bring back?

In personA clean break and a big, immersive week.

OnlineA practice already woven into your days.

If home is loud, or you know you'll check your email, come in person. Otherwise, online works just as well, and it's a great option if you want the flexibility.

Parents land on both sides. For some, two weekends are harder to clear than five weekdays. For others, a week away is the harder ask. Your call is the place to think it through.

06

If you do it from home

People who do well from home mostly do the same few things.

  • Tell the people you live with your hours. Undisturbed time on the retreat days; meals together are fine.
  • Keep one person you can call if something comes up, so you don't have to check your phone for it.
  • Put your phone away. Recordings and the course library work without it.
  • Plan meals ahead so cooking doesn't eat into the weekends.
  • Have more than one spot to sit, and somewhere to walk.
  • Clear the weekends completely. No half-days, no plans in the evenings.

Online, but away. Some people book a cabin or a rental near nature for the retreat weekends and join from there. You need a solid internet connection, and you get some of the "leave your life behind" feeling without taking the week.

What if I'm not in the US?

Sessions run on US Pacific time. The format works best from the Americas, Europe and Africa; when enough people are in Europe, a European facilitator runs a morning session there. From Asia the hours are hard, and we usually don't recommend it.
