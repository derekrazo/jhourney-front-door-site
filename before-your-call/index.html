The call

A conversation, not a pitch.

You'll talk with one of our facilitators: someone who knows the retreats from the inside. The point is to work out whether this fits you, and to answer whatever you want to ask.

What we'll ask you

  1. 1What brought you here. And why now. Most people have been curious for a while before they book a call.
  2. 2Your experience with meditation. Any amount is fine, including none.
  3. 3What you'd want to be different. What a good outcome would look like for you.
  4. 4Timing and format. Whether a week away or the online retreat around your work suits your life better.

What you can count on

  • There's no pressure to decide on the call.
  • Ask anything, including the awkward questions.
  • If a retreat isn't right for you, we'll say so. We ask everyone about their mental-health history before they book, for that reason.

Three things to think about

Bring these, and the call gets more useful.

Nothing to write down. A minute with each before the call is plenty.

  1. 01

    What made you look into this now?

    People often want something like this for a year or more before they act. Something usually tips it: a hard stretch, a friend who came back different, a window in the calendar. Knowing yours helps us talk about what matters to you.

  2. 02

    If it worked, what would be different?

    A month later, what would you notice? Enjoying ordinary days again, a practice you look forward to, a quieter inner voice. The clearer the picture, the more honestly we can say whether a retreat gets you there.

  3. 03

    What would stop you?

    Time off, timing, doubt that it'll work for you. Whatever it is, bring it. It's the most useful thing to talk about, and there's a straight answer for most of them below.

Jhana, in one minute

Deep joy and calm, learned on purpose.

Jhanas are states of deep pleasure and calm that most people don't know exist. They're a skill, not a gift: you can learn them.

It isn't a feat of concentration. It's closer to noticing a pleasant feeling, relaxing into it and letting it grow until it fills your attention. A retreat gives you the time, the method and a teacher reading your progress every day.

The usual doubts

Four questions almost everyone asks.

Straight answers, with the numbers behind them. Each links to a longer answer.

What if I'm in the 40% who don't reach jhana?

It happens: about 4 in 10 people don't reach jhana during the retreat. Many still leave with something they value, but not all of them, and they rate the week lower.

36.1% of people who didn't reach jhana still called the retreat life-changing (140 of 388)

The longer answer →

Will it last once I'm home?

The afterglow fades, usually within days or weeks, and we tell everyone to expect that. What lasts is the skill, and it lasts the way any skill does: with practice.

50.9% of people who reached jhana left able to get back in reliably, in under an hour (329 of 647)

How alumni keep it →

I can't take a week off.

That's what the online retreat is for: two retreat weekends with live teachers and your normal workdays in between. You take two Fridays off, and the results are close to in person.
